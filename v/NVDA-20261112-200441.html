How it is computed

Price in five years = next-year earnings × (1 + growth)5 × the P/E in five years. My valuation takes the analysts' consensus growth. Up to 20% a year it holds today's P/E, so the price grows as fast as earnings. Faster growth does not last, so above that the P/E in five years moves from today's toward the market's 19.2×, all the way at 50% a year; a P/E already under the market's is kept. The result is capped at +500%.

The stress test corrects two things. Five-year growth forecasts run hot: in Chan, Karceski & Lakonishok (2003) the most optimistic fifth of companies was forecast to grow 22.4% a year and delivered 9.5%, so growth here is 1% + 0.42 × the forecast. And a multiple above the market's is assumed to drift back toward it; one below it is not assumed to rise.
